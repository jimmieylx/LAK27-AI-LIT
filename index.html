01 / The workshop

Learning AI literacy
from what people do.

AI literacy is increasingly recognised as an essential educational capability. Yet evidence of what people know about AI does not fully capture how they reason, judge and regulate their interactions with it.

The second AI-LIT workshop focuses on enacted AI literacy: the observable decisions learners make when seeking assistance, checking claims, integrating AI contributions, delegating tasks and retaining oversight. We will examine how learning analytics can make these processes interpretable and connect them with learning, human agency and intervention.

02 / A research agenda

From capabilities
to credible evidence.

The workshop connects what AI literacy is intended to mean with what can actually be observed in human–AI interaction, and with the outcomes that matter for learners.

01 — CAPABILITY
◎

What matters?

Understanding, judgement, critical use and control of assistance.

02 — TRACE
⌁

What happens?

Requests, checks, edits, rejections, handovers and revisions.

03 — INFERENCE
◇

What does it mean?

Interpreting process data with validated analytical approaches.

04 — OUTCOME
↗

What develops?

Learning, transfer and appropriate human agency.

05 — ACTION
✳

What can improve?

Feedback, scaffolds, assessments and interventions.

A shared research canvas, not a prescriptive assessment model. Participants will challenge, refine and extend these connections.

03 / Areas of inquiry

Questions worth
working on together.

We welcome work across learning analytics, artificial intelligence in education, assessment, learning sciences, educational psychology and human–computer interaction.

01

AI literacy as lived practice

How do learners enact AI literacy through goal-setting, help-seeking, checking, editing, selective uptake and reflection in authentic tasks?

ProcessesHuman–AI interactionContext
02

Process-based assessment

How can learning traces extend self-report and performance-based measurement, with appropriate evidence of validity?

03

Human agency and delegation

When do learners retain, share or relinquish decisions—and what evidence suggests productive oversight?

04

Learning beyond performance

What can learners explain, reconstruct or transfer after AI assistance changes or disappears?

05

Scaffolding and intervention

How might analytics-informed support promote independent judgement, responsible reliance and continued capability development?

06

Equity, ethics and longitudinal development

How can assessment account for diverse settings, access and experience, while protecting learners’ privacy and agency over time?

FairnessResponsible useLongitudinal change
04 / Call for contributions

Studies, process-based measures, trace analyses, validation approaches and experimental or longitudinal evidence.

↗COLLABORATIVE DESIGN LAB

Connect the evidence to the learning claim.

Groups will work through realistic cases of human–AI collaboration and specify six elements:

  1. AI literacy capability
  2. Observable interaction trace
  3. Analytical method and validity check
  4. Learning or transfer outcome
  5. Implications for human agency
  6. Assessment or intervention design

Collective output
An open, co-developed AI Literacy in Action research roadmap.
